This building of humble origin is located in the Templete Square of Caravaca de la Cruz (Murcia) and, according to oral tradition, was the home that Saint John of the Cross inhabited during his travels to the town for the foundation of the Discalced Carmelite convent. Historical documentation indicates that the mystical saint rented "a small house from some Moriscos" in 1586 near the Church of the Immaculate Conception.

## The Building

The property is catalogued in the Urban Development General Plan of Caravaca de la Cruz with grade 2: structural conservation. It has three floors and is a good example of humble residential architecture that has not undergone significant changes since the 18th century: wooden paneled joinery and the only wooden projecting balcony preserved in Caravaca de la Cruz remain.

## The Intervention

{% media(align="left") %}
![proyecto 1](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/00746d3d-5b8e-4636-835e-b4b169fbcafe)

In 2020, the facades and roof were intervened, but the interior was in very poor condition. In 2021, the project to rehabilitate the property and convert it into the House Museum of Saint John of the Cross in Caravaca began. The applied criteria were maximum respect for the building's interior and its historical and cultural values, and the use of low environmental impact techniques and materials. The program established by the Conde Santa Ana de las Torres Foundation, owner of the building, consisted of reproducing the historical and ethnographic values on the ground floor, generating exhibition rooms on the first floor that would bring the life of Saint John of the Cross and his relationship with Caravaca closer to the visitor, and a library and meditation room on the top floor. To accomplish this, a new volume was built next to the courtyard to house an elevator, evacuation staircase, and toilets, in such a way that the historic building would have minimal impact from new installations.
{% end %}

{% media(align="right") %}
![interior 1](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/624159a2-8a33-43de-b1c3-6ef4c0628dda)

Thus, on the ground floor, there is a small office outside the museum route, and the rest of the space recreates a humble 16th-century dwelling. To this end, it was proposed to recover the fireplace and furnish the main room accordingly. In the area where animals were kept, half of the earthen floor was left unpaved, just as it had remained until our days. The original staircase follows the humble dwelling model of the area: located on the side behind a partition and made entirely of plaster, a finish that was respected in the project, since a new staircase in the additional volume would be the one leading to the upper floors.
{% end %}

{% media(align="left") %}
![Rehabilitated Interior of the House Museum](/images/trabajos/casa-san-juan-cruz/sando-trabajo-san-juan-2.jpg)

In the wall separating the first from the second bay, an opening was created, and in the wall separating the second from the third, an old blocked opening was utilized in such a way as to guarantee a clear and obstacle-free route for people with reduced mobility.

A fundamental issue emphasized in the project was respect for the original fabric, with its irregularities and volatile geometry, so that cornices and profiled edges were avoided. Ultimately, it was about preserving the essence of the property.

{% end %}
