This building of humble origin is located in the Templete Square of Caravaca de la Cruz (Murcia) and, according to oral tradition, was the dwelling that San Juan de la Cruz inhabited during his travels to the town for the foundation of the Discalced Carmelite convent. Historical documentation indicates that the mystical saint rented "a small house from some Moriscos" in 1586 near the Church of the Immaculate Conception.

## The Building

The property is listed in the Urban Planning General Plan of Caravaca de la Cruz with grade 2: structural conservation. The catalog sheet indicates the following in observations: "house where San Juan de la Cruz lived". It has three floors and is a good example of humble residential architecture that has not undergone significant changes since the 18th century: the wooden panel joinery and the only wooden projecting balcony preserved in Caravaca de la Cruz remain.

## The Intervention

{% media(align="left") %}
![proyecto 1](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/00746d3d-5b8e-4636-835e-b4b169fbcafe)

In 2020, the facades and roof were intervened, but the interior was in very poor condition. In 2021, the project to rehabilitate the property and convert it into the House Museum of San Juan de la Cruz in Caravaca began. The applied criteria were maximum respect for the building's interior and its historical and cultural values, and the use of low environmental impact techniques and materials.

{% end %}

The program established by the Conde Santa Ana de las Torres Foundation, owner of the building, consisted of reproducing the historical and ethnographic values on the ground floor, creating exhibition rooms on the first floor to bring the life of San Juan de la Cruz and his relationship with Caravaca closer to visitors, and a library and meditation room on the top floor. To achieve this, a new volume was built next to the courtyard to house the elevator, evacuation staircase, and restrooms, in such a way that the historic building would have minimal impact from new installations.

{% media(align="right") %}
![interior 1](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/624159a2-8a33-43de-b1c3-6ef4c0628dda)

Thus, on the ground floor, there is a small office outside the museum route, and the rest of the space recreates a humble 16th-century dwelling. To this end, it was proposed to recover the chimney and provide the main room with appropriate furniture. In the space where animals were kept, half of the floor was left with earth, unpaved, as it had remained until our days. The original staircase follows the model of a humble dwelling in the area: located on the side behind a partition and made entirely of plaster, a finish that was respected in the project, as a new staircase in the additional volume would lead to the upper floors.
{% end %}

{% media(align="left") %}
![Rehabilitated Interior of the House Museum](/images/trabajos/casa-san-juan-cruz/sando-trabajo-san-juan-2.jpg)

In the wall separating the first from the second bay, an opening was made, and in the one separating the second from the third, an old opening that had been blocked was used in such a way as to ensure a clear and obstacle-free route for people with reduced mobility.

A fundamental issue emphasized in the project was respect for the original fabric, with its irregularities and volatile geometry, so as to avoid cornices and defined edges. Ultimately, it was about preserving the essence of the property.

{% end %}
