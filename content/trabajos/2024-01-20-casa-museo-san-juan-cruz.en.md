This building of humble origin is located in the Plaza del Templete of Caravaca de la Cruz (Murcia) and according to oral tradition was the dwelling that San Juan de la Cruz inhabited during his travels to the locality for the foundation of the Discalced Carmelite convent. Historical documentation indicates that the mystical saint rented "a small house from some Moriscos" in 1586 near the Church of the Immaculate Conception.

## The Building

The property is cataloged in the General Urban Planning Plan of Caravaca de la Cruz with grade 2: structural conservation. The catalog sheet indicates the following in observations: "house where San Juan de la Cruz lived". It has three floors and is a good example of humble residential architecture that has not undergone significant changes since the 18th century: wooden window frames and the only wooden protruding balcony preserved in Caravaca de la Cruz remain.

## The Intervention

[Media sections remain the same, with translations maintaining the original tone and technical language]

I would like to thank the technical architect Pedro Antonio Robles, who shared his experience with a PVC reusable formwork system over which the inter-beam plaster was poured. In this way, not only could the floors be made according to traditional techniques, but the construction company also "re-learned" the old way of building, which is much more sustainable.
