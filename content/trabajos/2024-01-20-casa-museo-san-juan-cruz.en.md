This building of humble origin is located in the Templete Square of Caravaca de la Cruz (Murcia) and, according to oral tradition, was the dwelling that San Juan de la Cruz inhabited during his trips to the town for the foundation of the Discalced Carmelite convent. Historical documentation indicates that the mystical saint rented "a small house from some Moriscos" in 1586 near the Church of the Immaculate Conception.

## The Building

The property is listed in the Urban Planning General Plan of Caravaca de la Cruz with grade 2: structural conservation. It has three floors and is a good example of humble residential architecture that has not undergone significant changes since the 18th century: the wooden panel carpentry and the only wooden cantilevered balcony preserved in Caravaca de la Cruz remain intact.

## The Intervention

In 2020, the facades and roof were intervened, but the interior was in very poor condition. In 2021, the project to rehabilitate the property and convert it into the San Juan de la Cruz House Museum in Caravaca began. The applied criteria were maximum respect for the building's interior and its historical and cultural values, and the use of low environmental impact techniques and materials.

{% media(align="left") %}
![Rehabilitated Interior of the House Museum](/images/trabajos/casa-san-juan-cruz/sando-trabajo-san-juan-2.jpg)

The result of the intervention shows the care taken to preserve the essence of the historic building, maintaining original elements such as wooden beams and traditional lime finishes.

The recovery of interior spaces has allowed the creation of a museum route that connects visitors with 16th-century daily life, respecting the humble scale of the original building and its atmosphere of spiritual retreat.

In this way, the ground floor houses a small office outside the museum route, and the rest of the space recreates a humble 16th-century dwelling. To this end, the fireplace was to be restored and the main room furnished accordingly. In the area where animals were kept, half of the earthen floor was left unpaved, just as it had been preserved to this day.
{% end %}

The program established by the Conde Santa Ana de las Torres Foundation, owner of the building, consisted of reproducing the historical and ethnographic values on the ground floor, creating exhibition rooms on the first floor to bring visitors closer to the life of San Juan de la Cruz and his relationship with Caravaca, and a library and meditation room on the top floor.

To accomplish this, a new volume was built next to the patio to house the elevator, evacuation staircase, and bathrooms, ensuring minimal impact of new installations on the historic building. This volume was designed with a white façade made of lime mortar to appear neutral in relation to the adjacent historic building and guarantee a contemporary reading that would differentiate itself from the pre-existing adjacent façade, while reproducing the triple-row tile eaves to achieve proper integration.

Inside, the joists were designed in the same way they had been constructed for centuries in the area's houses, using round logs and gypsum vaulting. The ground floor space dedicated to kitchen and living room stands out, reproducing the lifestyle of a humble 16th-century dwelling.
